Problem

What the team learns from past disputes lives in people’s heads

Tori knows QuickBox pushes back on remote-area fees. Sam, who joined in the spring, doesn’t. Nothing in the app answers “what should I know before disputing this?”

The only history today is Credit outcomes. It has a 12-column finding ledger with the reason hidden in an expandable row, and a Biller table whose collection rate is by amount and counts waiting disputes as failures (L5, L7). Denial reasons are optional, and the app only asks for one after Denied is saved (L4).

What the meeting asked for

Newly confirmed · direction

Turn denial reasons and outcomes into reusable knowledge, “eventually”.

Compatible additionwithin one accountPotential conflictacross customers (C4, n347)

It came with two limits: no recommendation engine, and no weak pattern presented as fact. In the recommended order (plan H), 06 comes last because it can only be as good as 04’s captured answers and 05’s definitions.

Five ways guidance goes wrong

Small samples

Most accounts have only a few answered disputes per Biller and charge type. The demo has about 4 answers across 3 Billers (plan A1), so every Biller × type pair has fewer than 3. Existing

Answer: tiers, and nothing below 3.

Correlation isn’t cause

“The 3 that included the rate sheet were paid” doesn’t prove the rate sheet did it. Those disputes may have been bigger, newer, or handled by someone else.

Answer: past tense, counts, and “Past results, not a promise.”

Stale history

Contracts, rate cards and the people at a Biller change. A 2025 denial may say nothing about 2026.

Answer: a 12-month window and a date on every note.

Steering people away from valid claims

The overcharge is owed whatever QuickBox did last time. A “3 of 4 denied” note next to a checkbox pushes people to untick it.

Answer: no early signals at the points where people choose what to include (F9), and no pre-selection (D6).

Weak inputs

The answer date is the day someone recorded it (L3). Reasons are optional and asked for late (L4). Implentio may start recording answers (C11). The system may not invent reasons (n350).

Answer: no claims about response time, the source always named, and reasons quoted word for word.

Earlier idea · 2026-09-24 improvement plan, “Later” column

“‘Usually accepted’ hints when choosing findings, e.g. ‘QuickBox credited base-freight findings 9 times out of 10’.” Principle: “Recommendations only on trustworthy inputs. ‘Usually accepted’ hints only once there’s enough outcome data.”

Kept: the principle, which becomes the confidence tiers below. Dropped: hints “when choosing findings”, a Potential conflict with D6 (no pre-selection) and with the plan review’s F9 rule. Also dropped: “9 times out of 10”, which reads as a forecast. This file uses counts with a window instead (“8 of your last 9 · Mar – Sep 2026”).

3d · Hard rules

Proposed · extends plan F6

Never

  • ✕Predictions: “likely to be approved”, “87% chance”.
  • ✕Percentages in guidance. Counts only (see money vs counts below).
  • ✕Traffic-light verdicts, risk scores, red or green chips.

Always

  • ✓The count: “3 of your 4”.

  • ✓The time window, by the month each dispute was sent.
  • ✓A link to the cases: “See the 4 disputes”.
  • ✓The source label, and who recorded each quote.

  • ✓“Past results, not a promise.” under every sentence.
  • ✓Plain charge names from finding-copy.ts, the same words as the card title.

Money vs counts: two true numbers that can seem to disagree

ProposedWording · Q-06i

Guidance counts cases. Credit outcomes’ recovery rate is by amount: recovered ÷ amount disputed and answered (C5). Both can be true at once and still look contradictory. For example, Flowspace denied 3 of 4 fuel-charge disputes, but the one it paid was $1,050.00 of the $1,240.00 disputed, an 85% recovery rate.

  • A guidance sentence never shows a percentage, so there are never two conflicting percentages on screen.
  • Credit outcomes shows both side by side: “1 of 4 disputes paid · $1,050.00 of $1,240.00 recovered”.
  • If counts and money point opposite ways, the sentence adds one clause: “…1 was paid in full, and it was the largest ($1,050.00 of the $1,240.00 disputed).”
