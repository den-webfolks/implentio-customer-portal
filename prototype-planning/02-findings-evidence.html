Showing why a charge is wrong ("Show me why")

When Implentio tells a customer "your Biller overcharged you $9,322", the customer's first question is "how do you know?" This page plans how we answer it: a short answer first, the reasoning if they want it, and every single package if they really want to check — without burying anyone in giant tables.

The story in one minute

Who's involved

A brand (our customer) sells products online. A Biller — a warehouse company such as QuickBox — packs and ships its orders and sends it a bill. The packages are driven by a carrier such as UPS or DHL. Implentio checks every line of that bill against the prices the brand agreed to in its contract.

What Implentio finds

Implentio checks every package (one box sent to one person) on the Biller's invoices. Each check produces a credit memo: a list of findings. A finding is a group of packages with the same main mistake, e.g. "Fuel charge higher than your contract allows · 172 packages · $35.10". Each package is in one finding only. The customer can dispute a finding: email the Biller and ask for the money back.

The problem

Before asking for money back, people want proof. Today the app shows one example package and a general sentence. The client says customers don't fully trust the number, and the full package table is overwhelming.

What this page proposes

Three steps of detail, each answering one question: What did you find? → Why do you think so? → Show me every package. Most people stop at step 1 or 2. Where step 3 opens (a pop-up, a page, a side panel…) is still undecided.

Words used on this page

The customer never sees this list. Instead, the same explanations live inside "Show me why", right where each word appears, and use that package's own numbers — try tapping a charge in section 4. Proposed

Package
One box sent to one person: one order, packed at the warehouse, delivered by a carrier. It has its own tracking number and its own lines on the bill.
Invoice
The Biller's bill: a long list of packages, each with its charges. A credit memo checks all the invoices for a period.
Finding
A group of packages with the same main mistake, e.g. "Fuel charge higher than your contract allows · 172 packages · $35.10". Each package is in one finding only, and the finding's amount includes every charge on its packages. The customer disputes findings.
Contract price / rate card
The price list the brand and the Biller agreed on. Every "should have cost" number comes from it.
Shipping price industry: base freight
The main price for moving a package. Depends on speed, distance and weight.
Fuel charge fuel surcharge
An extra percentage on top of the shipping price. The percentage changes weekly with a published fuel index.
Home-delivery fee residential surcharge
An extra fee for delivering to a home instead of a business.
Remote-area fee DAS, delivery area surcharge
An extra fee for hard-to-reach ZIP codes. Only allowed for ZIPs on the carrier's official list.
Zone
How far the package traveled, as a number: 2 is nearby, 8 is across the country. Farther costs more.
Service level
How fast it was sent: Ground, 2nd Day Air, Next Day Air…
Below contract
The Biller charged less than the contract on one part of a package. We compare each package's whole bill with its whole contract price, so this already makes that package's difference smaller. It's never a separate credit.

What's wrong today

The amount is a mix of charges, and we don't say so

The biggest finding is called "Shipping price higher than your contract rate · $9,322.20". But that $9,322 is really $9,003 extra shipping price + $1,323 extra fuel + $5 extra remote-area fees, minus $1,009 where the Biller charged less than the contract. We have this breakdown in the data; the screen just never shows it (it should be shown charge by charge, never as one sum minus another).

One finding's name says the opposite of its numbers

"Home-delivery fee charged incorrectly · $22.64" — yet the home-delivery fees on those packages were $48 below the contract. The extra money actually came from remote-area fees and shipping price. Anyone who looks closely will stop trusting the rest.

We never say where the "correct" price came from

Customers ask "says who?". We answer with one general sentence per finding. We can't yet name the exact price list and its date, which week's fuel percentage we used, or which ZIP list we checked against. Q-E2

The hard cases are hidden

5 of the 6 demo memos have no package details at all. On the main memo, 6 bills Implentio couldn't check (a UPS price list was missing) are never shown. And the full package table has 26 columns — the PM called it "a lot".

Ask the client first (Q-E1)The app has already improved since the prototype the client saw: today a finding card opens "Show me why" in place, and the package list opens in a pop-up showing only the charges that differ. Did the client react to this, or to the old prototype's nested tables? What were they trying to find and couldn't? The answer decides how much of this plan we need.
Rules we won't break
  • Plain words first; the industry term only as a small label.
  • An example is always labeled as an example, never passed off as typical.
  • The math always reads the same way: billed − contract = difference.
  • We say what was different, never guess why. The top package's shipping price was exactly double the contract, but we don't write "they used the wrong price list" — we can't prove it.
  • We never show a total of what the Biller undercharged. The customer doesn't owe it back, and showing it invites confusion.
  • If we don't have a document, we say "not attached" — never a made-up link.

One example

One package was billed $223.50; under your contract it should have been $117.39.

A Zone 107, 16 lb, Next Day Air Residential package on invoice Q3098513 was billed $223.50 against $117.39 expected — a $106.11 net variance made up of base freight $92.93 unfavourable, fuel $18.90 unfavourable, residential $3.72 favourable, other $2.00 favourable.

How we worked it out

Each expected charge was rebuilt from the package's service level, zone and billable weight using the contracted rate table, then compared with every charge billed on that package.

  • The name says "shipping price", but $1,322.77 of the amount is fuel on the same packages — and the separate "Fuel charge" finding holds only $33.94 of the memo's fuel.
  • Nothing says the example is the most extreme package (billed at double), not a typical one.
  • "The contracted rate table" — which one? From when?
  • Can't tell if all 510 packages were a bit off, or a few were way off.
  • The grey sentence repeats the example in jargon ("Zone 107", "unfavourable", "net variance") and never says what those words mean.
Proposed: the same finding, with the new "Show me why" Proposed
  • The fuel isn't hidden any more: one equation (billed − your contract = overcharged), then every charge on one zero line. Shipping price and fuel add to it; the home-delivery and other charges billed below contract come off. A Chart / Table switch gives the exact values.
  • Few or many packages over? "Most were $15–$20 over; the largest was $106.11", with a histogram that marks the top 10%.
  • A typical package first: the example is a package slip (tracking, invoice, its details and charges), and the largest package is one tap away, clearly labeled.
  • No jargon to decode: tap "Shipping price", "Zone 107" or "Home" to see what it means for this package, with a small diagram and the word printed on the invoice.
  • How we checked replaces the second tab: the price list, the invoices and the reviewer, each saying honestly whether we have the file.

The plan: three steps of detail

Each step answers one question, and people stop at the one that answers theirs. Our earlier research found three kinds of customer; each maps to a step. Everything needed to act (tick and dispute) is in step 1 — steps 2 and 3 are for checking.

Step 1 · The finding card"What did you find?"The problem in plain words, what it covers (carrier, service level, charge type), the amount with its invoices and packages, and the dispute deadline as a chip. One action row: "Include in dispute package" as a bordered control, and "Show why". When the finding's name doesn't match where the money came from, the card says so. ProposedFor the "just handle it" customer: enough to tick and dispute.
Step 2 · "Show me why", on the card"Why do you think so?"One panel of section cards, all visible at once. What makes up the overcharge: billed − your contract = overcharged, then every charge as bars on one zero line, with a table twin. Overcharge per package: one sentence and a histogram (just the sentence for 25 packages or fewer). How one package was priced: a typical package as a slip, the largest one tap away; tap any charge or detail to see what it means, in the words printed on the invoice. How we checked: the method, the documents and the invoices, each saying whether we have the file. While you scroll the panel, a bar with the title, amount and "Hide why" stays at the top. ProposedFor the "let me check, then send" customer.
Step 3 · The package list"Show me every package."Every package, showing only the charges that differ. Search, filter by speed, "only where fuel differs", click a package to see how it was priced, full breakdown and download for experts. Zones show as printed on the invoice (e.g. 107). Doesn't repeat step 2.For analysts, and whoever answers the Biller's follow-up questions.

“$40 vs $20” = billed vs your contract. Made-up round numbers.

Step 2 · Why package A’s fuel went up
Your contract
shipping $20$5
Billed
shipping $40$10

Fuel is a percentage of the shipping price. The percentage was right (25%), but it was taken from a shipping price that was doubled. So the extra $5 of fuel isn’t a separate mistake — it’s caused by the shipping mistake, and it’s disputed together with it.

Step 3 · The findings the customer sees
Shipping price higher than your contract rate Packages A and C $31.25 made up of shipping +$25 and fuel +$6.25 — the fuel on these packages
Fuel charge higher than your contract allows Package B $1.00 the only package where fuel itself was the main mistake
Nothing counted twice: $31.25+$1.00=$32.25 = A $25 + B $1 + C $6.25, every package once.

In the real memo it’s the same story at a bigger scale: “Shipping price” (510 packages) shows +$1,322.77 of fuel that rose with the overcharged shipping price, while the separate “Fuel charge” finding (172 other packages) is about the fuel percentage itself.

Questions for the client

Q-E1 · What exactly confused customers?Was it today's app or the old prototype? What were they trying to find? This decides where the package list opens (A–E), or whether we only improve step 2.
Q-E2 · Which documents can Implentio attach?For each finding: the price list name, version and date? The fuel index week and percentage? The remote-area ZIP list? The contract clause? The files, or just their names? Without these, the "What we compared with" tab is mostly empty.
Q-E3 · Show the bills Implentio couldn't check?If yes, with what request to the customer ("send us the UPS price list")? Always kept out of the total? Hiding them is honest about the total, but customers never learn money might be there.
Q-E4 · Must the package list be shareable?With the Biller, a colleague, Implentio staff, later an AI assistant? If yes, options B or C.
Q-E5 · Package details for every memo?In real use, does every finding come with its packages, or only some memos (as in the demo)? Decides how often the "no package details" version appears.
Q-E6 · Who reviewed each finding?Is the reviewer and date recorded? We only show "Checked by Implentio" if it's true.
Q-E8 · How does Implentio decide which finding a package goes in? (for Implentio)In the demo memo 836 of 852 packages sit in the finding for their biggest difference, but 16 don't (e.g. a DHL package in "Fuel charge" whose fuel was below contract). What is the rule? "Show me why" tells customers how packages are assigned; it has to say the true rule.
Q-E7 · Findings whose name doesn't match the moneyShould Implentio group them differently ("Home-delivery fee" findings where the money came from other charges), or should the app explain it? Explaining works, but fixing the grouping avoids the doubt entirely.
Q-02a · Is the spread chart useful?Does it build trust, or is it noise for the "just handle it" customer? It's now behind "Show chart"; the one-sentence spread is always visible. If nobody opens it, drop it.
